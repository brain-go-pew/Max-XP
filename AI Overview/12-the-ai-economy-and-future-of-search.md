# 12 The AI Economy & Future of Search

SEO changes, zero-click searches, monetisation, market dynamics and where search goes next.

## 12.1 The AI Economy

The money flowing into building AI (chips, data centers, models) and the value it creates. Search is at the center: it's where the internet's biggest ad business meets AI's most popular use.

### Investment

US private AI investment ~**$286B in 2025** vs Europe ~$21B and China ~$12B (Stanford AI Index 2026).

### Adoption

Generative AI reached **53%** within three years.

### Consumer value

~**$172B a year** to US consumers by early 2026.

### The old trade

| The old trade | In return |
|---------------|-----------|
| Sites let Google crawl their content for free | Google sends visitors who see ads or buy |

| Now |
|-----|
| AI answers keep users on the page; search still needs content, but creators get fewer visits |

### Expanded context from Research Notes

The AI economy is the money flowing into building AI (chips, data centers, models) and the value AI creates for businesses and consumers. Search sits at the center because it's where the internet's biggest advertising business meets AI's most popular use: answering questions.

**Key numbers:**

- **Investment:** Stanford's 2026 AI Index reports US private AI investment of about $286 billion in 2025, far ahead of Europe (about $21 billion) and China (about $12 billion).
- **Adoption:** generative AI reached 53% adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet.
- **Consumer value:** the estimated value of generative AI tools to US consumers reached $172 billion a year by early 2026.
- **Scale of AI search:** Google says AI Overviews reach 2 billion+ monthly users and AI Mode passed 1 billion; ChatGPT has hundreds of millions of weekly users (see the AI Search notes).

**The core economic tension.** The old web ran on a simple trade: websites let Google crawl their content, and Google sent them visitors, who saw ads or bought things. AI answers keep users on the search page, so that trade is breaking. Search engines still need fresh content, but the creators of that content get fewer visits.

**Why it matters.** Whoever controls AI answers controls a huge share of online attention, advertising and shopping. This is why tech companies are investing hundreds of billions of dollars.

## 12.2 Zero-Click Searches and the Traffic Shift

### The numbers

| Measure | Finding | Source |
|---------|---------|--------|
| US searches with no click | ~45% (2016) → 60.45% (2024) → 68.01% (Jan–Apr 2026) | SparkToro / Similarweb |
| Clicks with an AI summary | 8% vs 15% without; 1% click a link inside | Pew Research |
| #1 result CTR with an AI Overview | Down ~58% (Dec 2025), up from a 34.5% drop in Apr 2025 | Ahrefs, 300,000 keywords |
| Lower positions | Position 2 lost about half its clicks; position 10 nearly 20% | Ahrefs |
| Google's share of traffic to 75,000+ sites | Down ~22% (June 2025 → May 2026) | Ahrefs via SparkToro |
| Publisher search referrals | Down ~33% globally, 38% in the US | Reuters Institute |

### Who is hit hardest

Simple informational content (definitions, how-tos, travel tips, recipes, health facts); brand and shopping searches less. A Berlin travel guide lost **70%** of its traffic after seven years of growth.

### Google's view

Overall traffic is stable and AI clicks are "higher quality"; independent studies mostly find fewer clicks.

### The risk

If creators stop publishing, AI has less fresh material.

### Expanded context from Research Notes

A **zero-click search** is one where the user gets what they need on the results page and doesn't click through to any website. It existed before AI (weather boxes, featured snippets), but AI Overviews have sped it up sharply.

**The numbers** (same table as above).

**Who is hit hardest.** Sites that answer simple informational questions (definitions, how-tos, travel tips, recipes, health facts). Brand and shopping searches are hit less. One Berlin travel guide reported losing 70% of its traffic to AI Overviews after seven years of growth.

**Google's view.** Google argues overall traffic is stable and clicks from AI results are "higher quality," with visitors more likely to stay. Independent studies mostly find fewer clicks.

**Why it matters.** Websites that depend on visits for ad revenue or sales are losing their audience, while AI tools still rely on their content. If creators stop publishing, AI answers have less fresh, reliable material to draw on.

## 12.3 From SEO to GEO

### Definitions

- **SEO:** ranking high in results.
- **AEO (answer engine optimization):** picked as a direct answer.
- **GEO (generative engine optimization):** cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews.

### The GEO paper (Princeton, Georgia Tech, Allen Institute for AI, IIT Delhi; KDD 2024)

~10,000 queries:

- Adding **quotations, statistics and citations** raised visibility up to **40%** (37% on Perplexity).
- **Keyword stuffing** barely helped.
- **Lower-ranked sites gained most.**

### Traditional SEO vs GEO

| Traditional SEO | GEO / AI search |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Goal:** Rank high in links | **Goal:** Be cited or mentioned in the answer |
| **Competes on:** One keyword | **Competes on:** Many fan-out sub-questions |
| **What helps:** Keywords, backlinks, page speed | **What helps:** Clear answers, stats, sources, expert quotes, authority, freshness |
| **Success:** Rankings, clicks, traffic | **Success:** Citations, brand mentions, share of voice |

### In practice

Direct answers near the top, original data and named experts, cover related sub-questions, keep pages updated, let AI crawlers in, build a brand people search for.

GEO is young and engines change often.

### Expanded context from Research Notes

**What it is.** Search engine optimization (SEO) means making pages rank high in search results. With AI answers, the goal shifts to being cited inside the answer. New names have appeared:

- **AEO (answer engine optimization):** shaping content so it's picked as a direct answer (snippets, voice, AI).
- **GEO (generative engine optimization):** getting content cited by generative AI like ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews.

**The research behind GEO.** A 2023 paper by researchers from Princeton, Georgia Tech, the Allen Institute for AI and IIT Delhi (published at KDD 2024) tested what makes AI search engines cite a source. Across about 10,000 queries:

- Adding quotations, statistics and citations to content raised visibility in AI answers by up to **40%**, and up to **37%** on Perplexity.
- **Keyword stuffing** (the old SEO trick) barely helped.
- **Lower-ranked sites gained the most**, suggesting GEO could help smaller publishers compete.

| Traditional SEO | GEO / AI search |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Goal:** Rank high in the list of links | **Goal:** Be cited or mentioned in the AI answer |
| **Unit of competition:** One keyword | **Unit of competition:** Many fan-out sub-questions |
| **What helps:** Keywords, backlinks, page speed | **What helps:** Clear answers, statistics, sources, expert quotes, authority, freshness |
| **Success measure:** Rankings, clicks, traffic | **Success measure:** Citations, brand mentions, "share of voice" in AI answers |

**What changes in practice:** write clear, direct answers near the top of the page; include original data and named experts; cover related sub-questions (because of query fan-out); keep pages updated; make sure AI crawlers can access the site; and build a brand people search for by name. Traffic is no longer the only success measure; being the trusted source the AI quotes matters too.

**Why it matters.** Marketing and publishing jobs are changing, and a new industry of GEO tools has appeared. But GEO is young: AI engines change often, and early results don't guarantee future visibility.

## 12.4 Monetisation: Who Pays for AI Answers?

### The problem

AI answers cost far more to produce than links, yet show fewer ads and send fewer clicks.

### Four models

| Model | How it works | Example (2026) |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| **Ads in answers** | Sponsored or labeled ads in or below the answer | Google scaling ads in AI Overviews/AI Mode (10 countries, multi-turn ads in US). ChatGPT tested ads from 9 Feb 2026; $1B run rate by 31 Aug |
| **Subscriptions** | Monthly fee for better models, higher limits | ChatGPT Plus/Pro, Google AI plans, Perplexity Pro |
| **Content licensing** | AI firms pay publishers | OpenAI and Condé Nast |
| **Pay-per-crawl** | Sites charge AI bots per page or use | Cloudflare blocks AI crawlers by default (since July 2025), pay-per-crawl beta |

### Google's balancing act (Q3 2026)

Search revenue grew **12%** to **$63.35B**, slower than **17%** the quarter before, even as queries grew fastest in five years. AI Mode earns less per query than classic search.

### OpenAI's ads

Ads appear below answers labeled "sponsored"; OpenAI says they don't influence answers; target **$2.5B for 2026**.

### Questions

Can users tell paid from organic? Will commercial pressure affect answers?

### Expanded context from Research Notes

**The problem.** AI answers cost far more to produce than a list of links (a large model runs for every query), yet they show fewer ad slots and send fewer clicks. Companies are testing four main ways to make money.

| Model | How it works | Example (2026) |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| **Ads in AI answers** | Sponsored products or labeled ads placed in or below the AI answer | Google is scaling ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode, expanding AI Mode ads to 10 countries and adding "multi-turn" ads in the US. ChatGPT began testing ads on 9 February 2026 and passed a $1 billion annualized run rate by 31 August |
| **Subscriptions** | Users pay monthly for better models and higher limits | ChatGPT Plus/Pro, Google AI plans, Perplexity Pro |
| **Content licensing** | AI companies pay publishers for access to their content | Deals between OpenAI and publishers such as Condé Nast; Cloudflare says blocking bots is pushing more AI companies to sign deals |
| **Pay-per-crawl / pay-per-use** | Websites charge AI bots per page crawled, or per use in an answer | Cloudflare blocks AI crawlers by default (since July 2025) and runs a pay-per-crawl beta |

**Google's balancing act.** In Q3 2026, Alphabet's search revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion, slower than the 17% of the previous quarter, even though search queries grew at their fastest rate in five years. Google's chief business officer said fast growth in AI Mode, which currently earns less per query than classic search, contributed to the slowdown.

**OpenAI's ads.** ChatGPT ads appear below answers with a "sponsored" label; OpenAI says they don't influence answers and advertisers can't see private chats. It is targeting $2.5 billion in ad revenue for 2026, though some analysts doubt it will hit that.

**Why it matters.** Ads inside answers raise new questions: can users tell paid from organic recommendations, and will commercial pressure affect what the AI says? Meanwhile, publishers want payment for the content AI relies on.

## 12.5 Market Dynamics

### Key players (2026)

| Player | Position |
|--------|----------|
| **Google** | ~89–90% of global traditional search; AI Overviews 2B+; AI Mode 1B+ |
| **OpenAI** | 1B+ weekly ChatGPT users; leading chatbot; now selling ads |
| **Microsoft** | ~5% of traditional search; Copilot in Windows and Office |
| **Perplexity** | AI-native answer engine with its own index |
| **Others** | Gemini app, Claude, DeepSeek, Baidu, Yandex, Naver |

### US v. Google antitrust

- **Aug 2024:** ruled an illegal search monopoly.
- **Sept 2025 remedies (Judge Amit Mehta):** no forced Chrome sale (citing AI competition), but exclusive distribution deals banned for 6 years and some index and interaction data shared with rivals.
- **Jan 2026:** Google appealed; DOJ and states cross-appealed. Ongoing.

### Forces

- Default placement drives usage.
- The huge cost of AI favors big companies.
- Publishers blocking crawlers could limit smaller engines.

### Expanded context from Research Notes

Search is still dominated by Google, but AI assistants are a new kind of competitor that traditional market-share data doesn't fully capture.

**Key players:**

| Player | Position (2026) |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Google** | About 89–90% of global traditional search (StatCounter); AI Overviews 2B+ monthly users; AI Mode 1B+ |
| **OpenAI (ChatGPT)** | More than 1 billion weekly users; the leading AI chatbot; now selling ads |

**Antitrust.** In August 2024, a US federal judge ruled Google had illegally monopolized online search. In the September 2025 remedies decision, Judge Amit Mehta did not force Google to sell Chrome, citing new competition from AI, but banned exclusive distribution deals for Search, Chrome, Assistant and the Gemini app for six years and ordered Google to share some search index and user-interaction data with qualified rivals (possibly including AI companies). Google appealed in January 2026, and the Justice Department and states cross-appealed seeking stronger remedies. The case is ongoing.

**Forces reshaping the market:**

- **Distribution matters:** default placement in browsers, phones and operating systems drives usage, which is why default deals were at the center of the case.
- **Cost of AI:** huge spending on chips and data centers favors large companies.
- **Content access:** publishers blocking AI crawlers or demanding payment could limit smaller AI search engines.

**Why it matters.** Whether AI search becomes more competitive or simply reinforces existing giants will shape prices, privacy, and what information people see.

## 12.6 Future Development: Links → Answers → Agents

### The shift

From "search for links" to "ask for answers" to "ask an agent to get it done."

### Agentic commerce milestones

| When | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| **Sept 2025** | OpenAI Instant Checkout in ChatGPT with Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP) |
| **Jan 2026** | Google Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target, Walmart |
| **Mar 2026** | OpenAI retires Instant Checkout for ordinary merchants: "discover in AI, buy on the retailer's site"; Shopify makes 5.6M stores discoverable in AI tools |
| **Autumn 2026** | Google agentic checkout in AI Mode and Gemini (early access); Universal Cart in US |

### Multimodal and conversational

AI Mode queries ~3× longer; 1 in 5 uses voice, images or documents; visual searches pass 50 billion a month.

### Conversational ads

Ads that answer follow-up questions.

### A new content deal

A paid content market, or a web split into open and walled-off sites.

### Personalization and memory

More relevant answers, more privacy concerns.

### Open questions

- Will publishers keep producing quality content?
- Will citations improve?
- Will regulators let a few firms control the gateway to information?
- Can ads coexist with trustworthy answers?

### Expanded context from Research Notes

**Where search is heading.** The shift is from "search for links" to "ask for answers" to "ask an agent to get it done."

**1. AI agents and agentic commerce.** AI assistants that research, compare and buy on your behalf. The path so far shows it's harder than expected:

- **September 2025:** OpenAI launched Instant Checkout in ChatGPT with Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP).
- **January 2026:** Google launched the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) with Shopify, Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart.
- **March 2026:** OpenAI retired Instant Checkout for ordinary merchants and shifted to a "discover in AI, buy on the retailer's site" model; Shopify made products from 5.6 million stores discoverable in ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode and Gemini.
- **Autumn 2026:** Google opened agentic checkout in AI Mode and Gemini in early access and launched Universal Cart in Search and the Gemini app in the US.

**2. Multimodal and conversational search.** Google says AI Mode queries are about three times longer than classic searches, one in five uses voice, images or documents, and visual searches pass 50 billion a month.

**3. Ads become conversational.** Ads that respond to follow-up questions and sponsored recommendations inside answers.

**4. A new content deal.** Licensing, pay-per-crawl and pay-per-use could create a paid market for content, or the web could split into open sites and walled-off premium ones.

**5. Personalization and memory.** Assistants that know your preferences give more relevant answers but raise privacy concerns.

**Open questions:** Will publishers keep producing quality content with less traffic? Will AI answers become more accurate and better at citing sources? Will regulators allow a few companies to control the main gateway to information? And can ads coexist with trustworthy answers?

**Why it matters.** For businesses, the goal shifts from ranking to being chosen by AI. For users, convenience grows, but so does the need to check where answers come from.

- The old crawl-for-traffic trade is breaking; zero-click hit **68%**.
